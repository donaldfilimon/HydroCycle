HC-IF-601 · single-cylinder hydrogen fixture · zero-dimensional cycle model

Hydrogen cycle, from crank angle to work

Independent teaching model · not the HydroCycle feasibility solver. Illustrative outputs, not engine validation or operating limits. Hydrogen supplies the chemical energy; water supplies none.

A slider-crank drives the volume, a Wiebe curve releases the hydrogen's energy, Woschni convection leaks some of it to the walls, the first law turns the rest into pressure, pressure becomes torque through the rod geometry, and torque integrates into work. Every number on this page is model output; nothing is measured.

The math, in the order the model runs it

  1. Slider-crank kinematics

    The piston pin sits on the rod, the rod on the crank pin. Project both onto the bore axis. With the rod angle β defined by sin β = (r/l) sin θ, the piston distance from TDC is

    s(θ) = r(1 − cos θ) + l(1 − cos β) = r(1 − cos θ) + l − √(l² − r² sin²θ)

    The renders labelled "legacy display fixture (infinite rod)" drop the rod term, which is the same as letting l → ∞ so that β → 0:

    s_legacy(θ) = r(1 − cos θ)

    The difference peaks at ±90° with value l − √(l² − r²) ≈ r²/(2l). For the fixture (r = 43 mm, l/r = 3.5) that is 6.3 mm of travel, so the curves look alike on a plot and differ where it matters: volume at a given angle.

    Differentiating once and twice with respect to θ (radians) gives the speed and acceleration shape functions the torque model needs:

    ds/dθ = r sin θ · [1 + r cos θ / √(l² − r² sin²θ)] d²s/dθ² = r cos θ + r²(cos 2θ)/√(l² − r² sin²θ) + r⁴ sin²θ cos²θ /(l² − r² sin²θ)^(3/2) v_p = ω · ds/dθ a_p = ω² · d²s/dθ² S̄_p = 2 S N / 60

    The second-order term in d²s/dθ² is what makes piston acceleration asymmetric between TDC and BDC (larger at TDC), a feature the infinite-rod model cannot show. The page evaluates d²s/dθ² by central difference of the analytic first derivative.

  2. Cylinder volume and its rate

    V(θ) = V_c + A·s(θ) dV/dθ = A · ds/dθ

    At θ = −10° the fixture gives V = 60.43 cc against V_legacy = 59.35 cc, a 1.08 cc gap (1.78 %). A 1.8 % volume error at spark becomes a 2.4 % pressure error under n = 1.35, because p scales as V−n. The check table above recomputes these to four digits.

  3. Trapped charge at intake-valve close

    Up to IVC the cylinder is open to the manifold and held at intake pressure and charge temperature; mass is whatever fits. At IVC it is frozen:

    n_total = p_in · V(IVC) / (R_u · T_0) R_u = 8.314 J/(mol·K)

    Hydrogen burns as H₂ + ½(O₂ + 3.76 N₂), so one mole of H₂ needs 2.38 mol of air. The hydrogen mole fraction and the masses follow from φ:

    y_H2 = φ / (φ + 2.38) m_H2 = y_H2 · n_total · 0.002016 kg/mol m_air = (1 − y_H2) · n_total · 0.02897 kg/mol M_mix = y_H2·2.016 + (1 − y_H2)·28.97 g/mol R_mix = R_u / M_mix

    Hydrogen takes up volume: at φ = 1 it is 30 % of the charge by moles, the volumetric-efficiency penalty of port-injected H₂. Available heat is limited by whichever runs out first (stoichiometric air–fuel ratio 34.3 by mass, lower heating value 120 MJ/kg):

    Q_total = min(m_H2, m_air / 34.3) · 120×10⁶ J/kg

  4. Burn fraction, Wiebe form

    Rather than resolving a flame, the model prescribes the mass fraction burned after spark θ₀ over a burn duration Δθ:

    x_b(θ) = 1 − exp[ −a · ((θ − θ₀)/Δθ)^(m+1) ] a = 6.908, m = 2 dQ_c = Q_total · (x_b(θ + dθ) − x_b(θ)) dx_b/dθ = a(m+1)/Δθ · ((θ−θ₀)/Δθ)^m · (1 − x_b)

    a = 6.908 makes x_b = 0.999 at the end of Δθ. m = 2 gives the usual S-curve. With φ = 0, Q_total is zero and this step does nothing, which is the "NO IGNITION IN THIS RUN: x_burned = 0" state of the fixture renders. Hydrogen's laminar flame speed is roughly five to eight times gasoline's at stoichiometry, so Δθ for H₂ is short (20–40°) near φ = 1 and stretches out lean; the slider spans that range but does not compute it.

  5. Wall heat transfer, Woschni correlation

    When the heat-loss toggle is on, convection to the walls is added with Woschni's 1967 correlation. Units: p in kPa, B in m, T in K, w in m/s; h comes out in W/(m²·K).

    h = 3.26 · B^−0.2 · p^0.8 · T^−0.55 · w^0.8 w = C₁ · S̄_p + C₂ · (V_d T_r / (p_r V_r)) · (p − p_motored) C₁ = 2.28, C₂ = 0 during compression, 3.24×10⁻³ after spark p_motored = p_r · (V_r / V)^γ reference state r taken at IVC A_wall = 2A + πB·(s + h_c) head + crown + exposed liner dQ_w = h · A_wall · (T − T_wall) · dt

    The C₂ term is Woschni's way of saying combustion-driven turbulence raises the gas velocity in proportion to how far pressure has risen above the motored trace. It is why heat loss is dominated by the 40° after spark, and why the energy-balance table shows Q_wall as a meaningful fraction of Q_total when the toggle is on.

  6. First law on the closed cylinder

    Between IVC and EVO the cylinder is a closed system. Energy in from combustion, energy out to the walls and through the piston, ideal gas with ratio of specific heats γ:

    dU = dQ_c − dQ_w − p dV, U = p V / (γ − 1) dp/dθ = (γ − 1)/V · (dQ_c/dθ − dQ_w/dθ) − γ · (p/V) · dV/dθ

    With dQ = 0 the second term alone integrates to p V^γ = const, so the unfired fixture is exactly a polytropic compression with n = γ = 1.35, which reproduces 19.98 bar at −10° from 1 bar at BDC. Temperature is read off the gas law, not integrated separately:

    T = p V / (m_total · R_mix)

    When the variable-γ toggle is on, γ follows a linear fit in temperature, γ(T) = 1.40 − 6.0×10⁻⁵ (T − 300), clamped to [1.25, 1.40], evaluated at the start of each step. That is a coarse stand-in for the real drop in γ as vibrational modes and dissociation switch on; it lowers peak pressure and peak temperature relative to a constant γ of 1.35; toggle it with the fired preset loaded to see by how much.

    Integration steps dθ = 0.5°: the adiabatic part is applied exactly as p_i = p_{i−1}·(V_{i−1}/V_i)^γ, then the step's net heat is added as (γ−1)·(dQ_c − dQ_w)/V_i; work uses trapezoidal p. During intake p is held at p_in; at EVO pressure drops to p_ex in one step and temperature follows an isentropic blowdown, T_ex = T · (p_ex/p)^((γ−1)/γ), then stays there through the exhaust stroke.

  7. Gas force to crank torque

    The force on the piston crown is gas pressure minus crankcase pressure (taken as 1 bar) over the piston area. It travels down the rod, tilted by β, and acts on the crank pin at radius r. Resolving:

    F_gas = (p − p_crankcase) · A F_rod = F / cos β (rod carries more than the axial force) τ = F_rod · r · sin(θ + β) = F · r · sin(θ + β) / cos β τ_gas uses F = F_gas τ_net uses F = F_gas − m_recip · a_p m_recip = piston + pin + small end

    The inertia term is why net torque at high speed swings negative around TDC even while gas pressure is high: the piston is being decelerated by the rod. The mean of τ over 720° must equal W_cycle / 4π, and the identities table checks that.

  8. Work, mean effective pressure, efficiency

    W(θ) = ∫ p dV cumulative from −360°, trapezoidal p W_closed = W(EVO) − W(IVC) gross indicated work W_pump = W_cycle − W_closed intake + exhaust strokes IMEP = W_cycle / V_d τ̄ = W_cycle / 4π P_ind = W_cycle · N / 120 (one power stroke per two revolutions) η_ind = W_cycle / Q_total (defined only when Q_total > 0)

    Sign convention matches the fixture plot: the intake stroke at 1 bar adds +50 J (p ΔV with ΔV = +500 cc), compression subtracts, so the unfired trace reaches about −136 J at −10°. With p_ex above p_in the pumping loop is negative.

  9. Closing the energy balance

    Over the closed period the first law must hold in integrated form, and the table above reports each term and the residual:

    Q_released − W_closed − Q_wall − ΔU = residual ΔU = Σ m · c_v(T_i) · (T_i − T_{i−1}), c_v = R_mix / (γ(T) − 1)

    ΔU is summed step by step with the same c_v the integrator used, because with a temperature-dependent γ the shortcut U = pV/(γ−1) is not a state function and would misreport the balance by about 5 % of Q. Done this way, the residual is only the difference between the exact adiabatic step and trapezoidal work, well under 1 % of Q in every preset; the table shows the live value.

  10. Two hydrogen-specific checks

    The flags row above evaluates two things the physical fixture exists to look at, in the crudest form that is still a real criterion:

    • Illustrative pre-ignition temperature comparison, not a validated safety limit. The unburned charge temperature at spark is compared with hydrogen's autoignition temperature, about 858 K in air. Hydrogen's minimum ignition energy is 0.02 mJ, an order of magnitude below gasoline's, so hot spots (exhaust valve, deposits) ignite it well before that temperature; the flag is a floor, not a guarantee.
    • Illustrative thermal NOx flag, not a validated emissions or safety limit. Zeldovich NO formation becomes fast above roughly 1800 K. If peak bulk temperature exceeds that, the flag turns amber; a single-zone bulk temperature under-reads the burned-zone temperature by several hundred kelvin, so the real threshold is crossed earlier than this flag suggests. Lean operation (φ ≈ 0.4–0.5) is where H₂ engines sit for exactly this reason.
